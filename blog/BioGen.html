Ocean Generator™

A floating, modular solar-thermal system that captures sunlight using optical collectors, transports photons through fiber-optic channels, and converts energy into electricity and freshwater — designed for open ocean deployment.

System Overview

Ocean Generator™ is not a single machine — it is a distributed energy field composed of floating collection pods, optical transport channels, sealed thermal cores, and shared electrical output infrastructure.

Exterior Floating Array

Each pod operates as an independent energy module while remaining connected to neighboring pods for redundancy, power sharing, and grid export.

The collector geometry is optimized for wide-angle solar intake, allowing energy capture throughout the day without mechanical tracking.

Interior Conversion Core

Captured sunlight is routed through thick fiber-optic glass channels, transporting photons directly into the base of a sealed boiler.

A final concentrating lens intensifies photon density, heating the boiler from below and creating controlled steam generation without exposing sensitive components to seawater.

Photon Concentration

The photon concentration stage is key to maintaining high thermal efficiency. Unlike standard photovoltaic systems, this approach maximizes photon density and minimizes energy loss during transmission.

This optical compression allows for compact thermal chambers and modular deployment across floating platforms without complex moving parts.

Steam Generation

The concentrated light beam heats the exterior surface of a sealed high-pressure boiler. Inside, gravity-fed clean water is rapidly vaporized into steam.

The system maintains pressure and thermal buffering using advanced composite materials and vented steam paths — optimizing both safety and energy conversion rate.

Power Output

Onboard battery arrays store excess energy, enabling steady power delivery to off-grid installations, microgrids, or coastal infrastructure.

All electrical systems are isolated and marine-rated for corrosion resistance, and managed by a self-correcting control module.

Water Output

Seawater enters the system through filtered inlets and undergoes multi-stage purification, including reverse osmosis, UV sterilization, and fine-particle membrane separation.

Clean water is then either routed to the boiler or stored in an internal reservoir for offloading as potable freshwater.

Open System Philosophy

While individual components may resemble known technologies, the integrated architecture — optical transport, floating modular deployment, and sealed conversion — represents a system-level innovation intended for open exploration, collaboration, and global deployment.
